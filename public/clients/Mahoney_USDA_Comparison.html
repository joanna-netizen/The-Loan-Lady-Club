Your USDA comparison, side by side

Hi Cynthia,

Here are the four scenarios we put together, all using USDA financing with no down payment and an $18,000 seller credit. USDA is a government backed loan program for eligible rural and suburban areas that allows 100% financing, meaning the full purchase price can be financed.

We are comparing two things at the same time. First, the price point: $300,000 versus $310,000. Second, how the seller credit is used. Option A puts nearly all of the $18,000 toward buying down your interest rate. Option B splits it, buying the rate down partially and covering your closing costs so you bring little to nothing to closing.

Buying down the rate means paying discount points (a form of prepaid interest) at closing in exchange for a lower interest rate for the life of the loan. You get a lower payment over time, and you spend more money upfront to get it. That trade is the whole decision here.

What the decision comes down to

Option A gives you a lower monthly payment, but it asks you to bring roughly $10,400 to $11,100 to closing. Option B keeps nearly all of your cash in your pocket, and your payment is about $100 higher each month.

The break even point is how long it takes for the monthly savings to pay back the extra cash you put in upfront. In these estimates, that is close to nine years. If you sell or refinance before then, Option B would have cost you less overall. If you keep the loan longer, Option A starts working in your favor.

On price, moving from $300,000 to $310,000 adds about $73 to $75 per month in either option, and about $660 more cash to close with Option A.

There is no wrong answer here. It depends on how much cash you want to keep in reserve after closing and how long you expect to stay in the home. That is exactly what we will walk through together.
